# Architectural Standards and Codes

## Scope

Architecture sits at the intersection of multiple engineering disciplines and
adds its own standards: accessibility (ADA), building planning (IBC),
energy (ASHRAE, IECC), sustainability (LEED), and fire/life safety
(NFPA 101). No single "architectural structural code" exists — architects
reference the structural, mechanical, and electrical codes that govern their
designs, plus the planning and accessibility standards unique to building
design.

## Key standards by category

### Accessibility (mandatory, legally enforced)
| Standard | Title | Access | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADA Standards (2010) | ADA Standards for Accessible Design | FREE (ada.gov) | Federal accessibility requirements for public accommodations |
| ICC A117.1 | Accessible and Usable Buildings and Facilities | Paywalled (ICC) | Technical accessibility requirements (referenced by IBC) |
| ABA Standards | Architectural Barriers Act Standards | FREE (access-board.gov) | Federal facility accessibility |
| FHA | Fair Housing Act Accessibility Guidelines | FREE | Multi-family housing accessibility |

The **ADA Standards (2010)** are free and the most commonly referenced for
public accommodations. ICC A117.1 is the technical standard referenced by
the IBC and state building codes.

### Building planning and energy
| Standard | Title | Access | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| IBC | International Building Code | FREE (online) | Height/area limits, occupancy, egress, fire resistance |
| IECC | International Energy Conservation Code | FREE (online) | Building energy efficiency (envelope, lighting, mechanical) |
| ASHRAE 90.1 | Energy Standard for Buildings Except Low-Rise Residential | Paywalled | Energy efficiency (referenced by IECC) |
| ASHRAE 62.1 | Ventilation for Acceptable Indoor Air Quality | Paywalled | Mechanical ventilation requirements |
| ASHRAE 55 | Thermal Environmental Conditions for Human Occupancy | Paywalled | Thermal comfort criteria |

### Sustainability (voluntary but often contractually required)
| Standard | Title | Access | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| LEED | Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design | Paywalled (usgbc.org) | Green building rating system (Certified–Platinum) |
| IgCC | International Green Construction Code | Paywalled (ICC) | Green building code (mandatory alternative to LEED) |
| WELL | WELL Building Standard | Paywalled | Health and wellness in buildings |
| Passive House (PHIUS) | Passive House Standard | Paywalled (phius.org) | Ultra-low energy building standard |
| Living Building Challenge | Living Building Challenge | Paygalled (ilfi.org) | Regenerative building standard |

### Fire and life safety
| Standard | Title | Access | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| NFPA 101 | Life Safety Code | FREE online view | Means of egress, occupant protection |
| IBC Ch. 7–10 | Fire protection, means of egress | FREE (online) | Fire resistance ratings, egress width, travel distance |

## ADA Standards — key provisions (free, commonly referenced)

The ADA Standards (2010) are the most accessible architectural standard.
Key provisions:

| Topic | Section | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Accessible route | §206 | At least one accessible route to all public spaces |
| Door clearances | §404.2.3 | 32" minimum clear opening width |
| Ramp slope | §405.2 | Maximum 1:12 slope; 1:16 preferred |
| Accessible toilet rooms | §213 | Accessible stalls, grab bars, clear floor space |
| Parking | §208 | Accessible parking spaces by total count |
| Signage | §216 | Room identification, directional signage |
| Reach ranges | §308 | Forward/reach ranges for wheelchair users |
| Protruding objects | §307 | Objects protruding from walls must be detectable |

## Known conflicts and cross-checks

| Conflicts with | Issue |
|---|---|
| State accessibility codes | Some states have accessibility codes that exceed ADA (e.g., California CBC Ch. 11B). State code governs when more restrictive. |
| ASHRAE 90.1 vs IECC | IECC references ASHRAE 90.1 as an alternative compliance path. Both achieve the same goal. |
| LEED vs IgCC | LEED is voluntary rating; IgCC is mandatory code. Projects may pursue both. |
| NFPA 101 vs IBC egress | Both cover egress. IBC and NFPA 101 are largely harmonized but not identical. |

## Jurisdiction notes

- **US (general):** ADA is federal law (enforced by DOJ). IBC is adopted by
  states with state amendments. IECC for energy.
- **California:** CBC (based on IBC) with CA accessibility amendments
  (Ch. 11B) that exceed ADA.
- **EU:** EN standards for accessibility (EN 81-70 for elevators, etc.);
  national building codes for planning.
- **LEED:** Voluntary but often contractually required by owners or required
  by local ordinance (some cities require LEED or equivalent).

## When to use

Use this reference when:
- Checking accessibility requirements (ADA, ICC A117.1).
- Determining building height/area limits (IBC).
- Specifying energy efficiency (IECC, ASHRAE 90.1).
- Designing egress (NFPA 101, IBC Ch. 10).
- Referencing sustainability rating systems (LEED, Passive House).
- Specifying ventilation (ASHRAE 62.1) or thermal comfort (ASHRAE 55).
