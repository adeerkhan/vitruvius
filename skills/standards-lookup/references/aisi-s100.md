# AISI S100 — North American Specification for the Design of Cold-Formed Steel Structural Members

## Scope

Design standard for cold-formed steel (CFS) structural members — thin sheet
steel bent into shapes (C-sections, Z-sections, tracks, studs, joists).
Covers walls, floors, roofs, and framing systems in low- and mid-rise
construction. Different from AISC 360 (which covers hot-rolled and built-up
shapes) — the thin walls of CFS members require different stability and
effective-width calculations.

## Access

- **Paywalled:** Full document requires AISI purchase.
- **Free companion:** AISI publishes free design aids and framing standards
  (e.g., AISI S240 for lateral design, S400 for seismic).
- **No API.**
- **Citation fallback:** Cite by chapter + edition.

## Organization (AISI S100-16)

| Chapter | Title | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| A | General | Scope, definitions, materials |
| B | Element design | Effective width, local buckling, distortional buckling |
| C | Members | Flexure, shear, compression, combined |
| D | Connections | Welds, bolts, screws, power-actuated fasteners |
| E | Testing | Test requirements for assemblies |
| F | Shear wall and diaphragm design | CFS-framed shear walls and diaphragms |

## Key concepts unique to cold-formed steel

- **Effective width:** Thin webs/flanges carry stress non-uniformly; only a
  portion is "effective." Must compute effective section properties.
- **Distortional buckling:** A unique CFS failure mode (flange-lip rotation)
  not present in hot-rolled shapes.
- **Screw connections:** CFS uses self-drilling screws, not bolts — different
  connection design rules.

## Known conflicts and cross-checks

| Conflicts with | Issue |
|---|---|
| AISC 360 | Hot-rolled vs cold-formed. Do not apply AISC 360 effective width rules to CFS. |
| AISI S240 | Lateral design (wind/seismic) of CFS framing. Supplements S100. |
| AISI S400 | Seismic design of CFS. Supplements S100 for seismic. |
| ASTM C955 | CFS material specification; referenced by S100. |

## When to use

Use this reference when:
- Designing cold-formed steel studs, joists, or tracks.
- Calculating effective width or checking distortional buckling.
- Designing CFS connections (screws, welds).
- Designing CFS-framed shear walls or diaphragms.
