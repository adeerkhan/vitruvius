# IEEE Standards (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers)

## Scope

IEEE publishes standards across electrical, electronics, computing, and
communications. The most relevant for engineering research are power systems
(IEEE 1547, 519), software engineering (IEEE 830, 1012, 12207), and
networking (IEEE 802 family). IEEE standards are accessed via IEEE Xplore,
which requires subscription — they are among the least freely accessible
engineering standards.

## Access

- **Subscription (IEEE Xplore):** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org — full text
  requires individual or institutional subscription.
- **Free preview:** Abstracts and limited preview are free. Some IEEE
  standards are open access (IEEE 802.11 Wi-Fi specs are free to download;
  IEEE 1801 UPF is free).
- **No public API** for paywalled content.
- **Free alternatives:** Many IEEE standards have public summaries or
  equivalent IEC/CENELEC standards. Use these when the full text is needed
  but unavailable.

## Mandatory-language conventions

- **"shall"** — mandatory
- **"should"** — recommended
- **"may"** — permitted
- **"can"** — possible (not an obligation)
- **Informative annexes** — not enforceable

## Known conflicts and cross-checks

| Conflicts with | Issue |
|---|---|
| NEC (NFPA 70) | IEEE 1547 governs DER interconnection; NEC Art. 690/706 govern installation. Both apply. |
| IEC standards | Many IEEE standards have IEC equivalents (e.g., IEEE 802.3 ≈ ISO/IEC 8802-3). IEC editions may differ. |
| UL standards | IEEE may reference UL listing requirements; UL governs equipment safety certification. |
| NERC standards | US bulk power system reliability. IEEE standards may complement but NERC standards are mandatory for utilities. |

## Jurisdiction notes

- **US:** IEEE standards are voluntary unless adopted by regulation or
  contract. IEEE 1547 is adopted by most US states for DER interconnection.
- **International:** IEC (International Electrotechnical Commission) standards
  dominate outside the US. Many IEEE and IEC standards are harmonized, but
  not all.
- **EU:** CENELEC adopts IEC standards as EN standards. IEEE standards are
  not directly enforceable in the EU unless adopted.

## Free alternative: open standards

For networking research, IEEE 802.3 (Ethernet) and IEEE 802.11 (Wi-Fi) are
free to download at https://standards.ieee.org/standard/. Use these directly.
For power standards, the free IEEE 1547 summary documents and state-specific
adoption guides are often sufficient for research questions.
