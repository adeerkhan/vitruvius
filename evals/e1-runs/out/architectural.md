# Facade and Enclosure Systems — Building Science Survey

Enclosure performance rests on four continuous control layers: bulk water, air, vapor, and heat. Cladding choice matters less than continuity through roofs, sills, parapets, and penetrations; most failures are interface discontinuities, not material failures.

## System families

- Face-sealed barriers rely on one weatherproof skin. They tolerate little movement or workmanship error.
- Drained, back-ventilated rainscreens separate cladding from the air/water barrier, drain the cavity, and vent it to dry. AAMA 509 classifies this family.
- Pressure-equalized rainscreens add compartmented, vented cavities; AAMA 508 sets test and pass criteria. "Pressure-moderated" is more accurate, since full equalization is rarely achieved.
- Curtain wall and window wall are fenestration-led, tested as manufactured assemblies, with opaque infill and interfaces detailed separately.

Masonry cavity walls and drained EIFS follow the rainscreen logic; EIFS drainage is tested by ASTM E2273.

## Verification, not description

Specify test evidence: air-barrier assemblies to ASTM E2357 (with penetrations); static water penetration to ASTM E331; dynamic to AAMA 501.1; exterior-wall fire propagation to NFPA 285, triggered under 2021 IBC §1402.5 for combustible WRBs above 40 ft and §2603.5.5 for foam plastic; material R-value to ASTM C518; envelope U-factors and continuous air barrier to ASHRAE 90.1 Section 5 (§5.4.3).

Hygrothermal behavior — winter condensation, inward vapor drive, drying — is climate- and assembly-specific and needs project simulation.

## What we did not find

No single standard governs whole-enclosure integration; performance is always assembly-specific. Standard scopes and code triggers were verified against publisher and code listings; project values remain unverified until test reports are read. Edition-sensitive: confirm the jurisdiction's adopted IBC and ASHRAE editions.
