**Question.** For a personal fall arrest system in US construction, what limits does
OSHA set on arresting force, free fall, and deceleration?
**Edition:** eCFR text of 29 CFR 1926 Subpart M, read 8 Nov 2026.
**Non-goals:** equipment selection, state-plan variants, general industry.

**Primary evidence.** I read §1926.502(d) directly from the eCFR content API
(Title 29, current). Tier 1 code text; no secondary summary supplied a value.

| # | Claim | Location | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Max 1,800 lb (8 kN) arresting force, harness | §1926.502(d)(16)(ii) | verified |
| 2 | Max 900 lb (4 kN), body belt | §1926.502(d)(16)(i) | verified |
| 3 | Body belts barred from fall arrest after 1 Jan 1998 | §1926.502(d) intro | verified |
| 4 | Free fall ≤ 6 ft (1.8 m), no lower-level contact | §1926.502(d)(16)(iii) | verified |
| 5 | Deceleration ≤ 3.5 ft (1.07 m) | §1926.502(d)(16)(iv) | verified |
| 6 | Anchorage ≥ 5,000 lb per worker, or SF ≥ 2 qualified-person design | §1926.502(d)(15) | verified |

**Inferred, not in the clause.** Required clearance above the lower level is not a
stated number in (d)(16). Adding the verified 6 ft free fall and 3.5 ft deceleration
to worker height and margin gives roughly 17–19 ft. That is design arithmetic,
marked `inferred`, varying with attachment height and lanyard type — not an OSHA
value, and not citable as one.

**Not checked.** State-plan adoptions, the Appendix C protocol for combined weights
at or above 310 lb, and pending rulemaking. Those are unconfirmed; every claim above
pins only to the text read.
