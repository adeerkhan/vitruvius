## Rival Hypotheses

**H1 — Deficient material capacity (material/quality).** Low strength, poor compaction, honeycombing. *Prediction:* cores below specified strength; one wide flexural crack at maximum moment. *Falsifier:* specimens meet specification; cracking distributed or diagonal. *Contrast:* H2: adequate capacity, excess demand.

**H2 — Underestimated demand (design/analysis).** Unconsidered load case, restraint, or thermal/shrinkage movement. *Prediction:* recomputed demand exceeds capacity at the crack, matching predicted maximum moment or restraint point. *Falsifier:* demand within capacity with margin. *Contrast:* H3: normal demand, construction-specific.

**H3 — Construction overload (process).** Props removed early, stacked material, lift point. *Prediction:* crack age matches a construction event; no corrosion inside; erection loads exceed design. *Falsifier:* crack predates construction loading, or load history within design. *Contrast:* H1/H2 predict service-life cracking.

**H4 — Restrained shrinkage or thermal movement (environmental).** Restraint with insufficient crack-control reinforcement. *Prediction:* regular crack spacing both faces, widths independent of live load, negligible closure on unloading. *Falsifier:* cracks appear only under load and close on removal. *Contrast:* H1–H3 predict load-driven cracking.

**H5 — Stress concentration (geometry).** Bar congestion, low cover, notch, penetration, or bearing. *Prediction:* crack initiates at the discontinuity, not midspan. *Falsifier:* origin remote from geometric features. *Contrast:* others predict global origin.

## Discriminating Tests
- Crack mapping and orientation (flexural vertical, diagonal shear, map pattern) separates H1–H3 from H4/H5.
- Cores, cover meter, rebar survey test H1 and H5.
- Recomputed demand against construction records tests H2 versus H3.
- Strain-gauged load test (opening versus load) tests H4 against load-driven mechanisms.
