## Prose Synthesis

The fatigue behavior of additively manufactured Ti-6Al-4V is dominated by defects: porosity, lack-of-fusion (LoF) features, surface roughness, and tensile residual stresses. Across L-PBF (SLM), E-PBF (EBM), and WAAM processes, the central finding is that AM Ti-6Al-4V exhibits **fatigue strength 20–40% lower than wrought** in the as-built condition, with significantly greater scatter [2, 6, 14].

**S-N data at 10⁴–10⁷ cycles.** For machined L-PBF Ti-6Al-4V at R = 0.1, fatigue strength at 2×10⁶ cycles ranges from ~500–700 MPa depending on specimen volume [2]. WAAM material shows an intrinsic fatigue limit (defect-free) of 540 MPa, dropping to ~400 MPa with porosity [1]. As-built SLM material shows roughly 30% lower fatigue strength (~290 MPa) than machined [2]. These values are broadly consistent with the S-N databases compiled by Cutolo et al. (2022) and the review by Li & Affolter (2024) [9].

**HCF/VHCF at 10⁷–10⁹ cycles.** VHCF data for AM Ti-6Al-4V is genuinely sparse. Uematsu et al. (2021) [8] provide the most systematic VHCF dataset, using rotating-bending tests with artificial defects (10–500 µm) and proposing modified Murakami-type design curves for 10⁸ cycles. Fu et al. (2023) [11] report 20 kHz ultrasonic VHCF at R = −1 and R = 0.7, finding subsurface defect-initiated failures with large scatter. Hejazi et al. (2024) [10] extend this to EBM material in the VHCF regime. No study reports a classical endurance limit (runout at 10⁷–10⁹ cycles) for AM Ti-6Al-4V with high confidence; the defect-driven nature means fatigue life continues to degrade beyond 10⁷ cycles [8].

**Kitagawa-Takahashi (KT) diagrams.** Biswal et al. (2019) [1] provide the most complete KT framework for AM Ti-6Al-4V, proposing a modified 3-region diagram with El Haddad parameters a0 = 52 µm (surface pores) and 88 µm (internal pores). Pessard et al. (2021) [3] identify a critical defect size (√area) of ~30 µm below which defects are not detrimental. Le et al. (2020) [2] confirm the critical LoF pore threshold at 50–350 µm.

**Post-processing.** HIP closes internal porosity to <0.01%, recovering fatigue strength to ~700 MPa at 2×10⁶ cycles [2]. Surface treatments are critical: shot peening and centrifugal finishing bring L-PBF fatigue strength to machined levels [5]; laser peening outperforms shot peening [6]. A key finding is that surface roughness alone is a poor predictor — subsurface defects beneath smooth surfaces can still cause premature failure [5]. Build orientation effects (0°/45°/90°) are measurable but secondary to porosity and surface condition [13].

**AM vs wrought.** At 10⁵–10⁶ cycles, HIPed + machined AM Ti-6Al-4V approaches wrought fatigue strength (~600–700 MPa). At 10⁷+ cycles, the gap widens due to subsurface defect-driven initiation [2, 8]. Wrought Ti-6Al-4V exhibits a classical endurance limit of ~500–600 MPa (R = −1); AM material does not show a clear endurance limit in the same sense [8, 12].

## Gap: VHCF Data (10⁸–10⁹ cycles)

VHCF data for AM Ti-6Al-4V is **genuinely sparse**. The literature is dominated by S-N testing in the 10⁴–10⁷ range. For 10⁸–10⁹ cycles, only Uematsu et al. (2021) [8] and Fu et al. (2023) [11] provide systematic data, both on L-PBF material. **No published VHCF dataset for EBM Ti-6Al-4V at 10⁸–10⁹ cycles was identified.** The defect-driven failure mode makes this regime particularly important — Murakami et al. (2021) [DOI: 10.1016/j.ijfatigue.2020.106138] show that the conventional fatigue-limit concept breaks down for defect-containing materials beyond 10⁷ cycles. This is a significant gap for damage-tolerant design of AM Ti-6Al-4V components in long-life applications (aerospace, biomedical implants).
